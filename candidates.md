# Dad joke candidates

Fresh candidates written from scratch. Each joke is at most 200 characters and is judged on pun quality, groan factor, and straight-faced delivery on the page.

1. "I started writing a book about two socks. It has a hole in the plot." — 68 chars. Pun quality: clean double meaning of a hole in fabric and a hole in a story. Groan factor: high; the turn lands in four words. Delivery: flat, literal, punchline last. Strong first draft.

2. "I asked my dentist for a cake recipe. He said the secret is plenty of fillings." — 79 chars. Pun quality: fillings means dental work and cake filling; the setup makes both readings obvious on the first read. Groan factor: high, with an absurd professional straight face. Delivery: natural across a dinner table, punchline last. Chosen for the final text: its double meaning is sturdier and its table delivery more natural.

3. "I bought an extra loaf of bread. My wife asked why. I said I'm trying to rise above my problems." — 96 chars. Pun quality: rise works for dough and self-improvement. Groan factor: solid, but the extra setup costs momentum. Delivery: conversational, ending on the pun.

4. "I promised to stop telling food puns. That promise broke like a cracker." — 72 chars. Pun quality: cracker is both snack and fragile thing. Groan factor: moderate; meta-joke about puns feels a little knowing. Delivery: tight but slightly self-aware.

5. "My son asked what a round trip is. I told him it's when you go around and then trip." — 84 chars. Pun quality: literal reversal of a common phrase, easy to grasp. Groan factor: reliable but familiar in shape. Delivery: classic parent-to-child cadence.

6. "I tried to fix the kitchen sink. I gave up. I'm all washed up." — 62 chars. Pun quality: washed up means defeated and covered in dishwater. Groan factor: moderate. Delivery: deadpan and very short, but the sink detail does little work.

7. "I bought a ruler yesterday. It doesn't measure up." — 50 chars. Pun quality: measure up is immediate. Groan factor: mild; the joke is almost too quick. Delivery: a father could say it without blinking, but it leaves no aftertaste.

8. "My daughter asked why the moon follows us. I said, 'It's just looking for a little space.'" — 90 chars. Pun quality: space means distance and personal room. Groan factor: gentle rather than painful. Delivery: warm, but the quoted speech softens the punchline.

9. "I wrote a joke about a calendar. It was dated the moment I told it." — 67 chars. Pun quality: dated means old and marked with a date. Groan factor: mild-to-moderate. Delivery: clean ending, though the meta framing is slightly clever for a dad joke.

10. "I told my wife I'd stop buying bananas. She said, 'That's a-peeling.'" — 69 chars. Pun quality: a-peeling carries both appealing and banana peel, but the spelling looks coy on the page. Groan factor: decent. Delivery: the punchline belongs to the wife, which weakens the father's straight face.
